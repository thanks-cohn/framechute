# ÆXIS bridge in Framechute: authored worlds agents can understand

**Status:** Proposal, not an implemented Framechute integration. Companion proposals in Tiled-223D specify the world/API path; SUBSTRATE specifies the workspace and stage fit. The [stage roadmap](../Stages/README.md) and [agent design](agentic_design.md) provide the surrounding vision.

## Why this belongs in a creator workspace

The creator should be able to keep a Tiled map, a Blender model, a few reference photographs, written descriptions and eventually a book near the world they are building. Drop one small map onto another, ask an agent for an expansive master geography, preview the result, fix a coast and go straight back to the source. A world can be small and intimate or spread the same detailed landmasses across large cheap open seas; the ocean between them is an implicit region and renderer rule, not millions of stored water tiles. The author can always inspect what an agent inferred versus what was supplied.

The current standalone Tiled-223D work supplies a browser flight prototype, ordinary Tiled JSON import, procedural sample terrain, a limited low-ground insertion path, and a narrow versioned agent/programmer world API for rectangular regions, preview, commit and undo. Multi-map composition, reading books and images, semantic face labeling, a complete Tiled round trip and Godot export remain future work. Framechute hosting does not make those capabilities appear by itself.

## Two creative entrances, one world truth

Tiled artists bring 2D maps and elevation companions; Blender artists bring GLBs. ÆXIS provides the common meaning: stable world/landmass/asset IDs, coordinates, height, footprints, ownership, adjacency, attachment points, permissions and collision truth. The browser view, Tiled export and later game-engine export derive from this versioned structure. A displayed curved or folded world is an artistic projection of a flat wrapping geography unless a future topology is explicitly chosen. The firmament and lighting are separately editable.

Offer a small **surface studio** for each imported GLB. Select a base, front, entrance, roof or connection point with simple quadrilateral/part selection first; later offer mesh polygon and volumetric segmentation. Store labels with asset hashes, durable part bindings, normals and local/world transforms. A model may propose labels after inspecting images and geometry; show uncertainty and let the artist correct its guesses. A Blender reexport must preserve verified bindings or surface unresolved changes, never attach “door” to an unrelated face by accident.

## The agent's footholds

### Toonworld: the drawing is the control surface

For artists who draw rather than model, let a character stay visually flat like a paper figure while a rig underneath carries joints, pivots, hit regions and depth anchors. The artist identifies the intended camera angle of each drawing, including front, back, side or a particular winding-back pose, polygon-selects visible regions and aligns each one with its matching rig part at that pose. Pair drawn landmarks and desired silhouette with camera framing, joint positions, facing and occlusion. Drag the visible hand, jaw, eye or torso to edit motion through that rig. At chosen moments a labeled part of the animated drawing can protrude into 3D, making the 2D Toonworld feel as though it is entering the spatial world. Bind selected art regions to depth limits, render order, collision and a return pose so the effect is repeatable and adjustable. A proposed **Toonworld Dynamics engine** would use multiple artist-authored angle-to-rig examples to transform the hidden rig until its visible rendition matches the chosen drawing. Unseen angles need a declared fallback or more authored art. This is proposed work, starting with a simple multi-view rigged example.

Collect those examples through an artist-first interface: draw simple colored lines or polygons on a front-view image to mark the same arm, head or torso across drawings; fit an adjustable skeleton or cage behind it. Let the rig change lengths, widths and shape for squash-and-stretch poses, then fit further drawings from other camera angles while preserving part identity. Each approved example pairs the drawing, segmentation, camera, fitted rig parameters, visible and hidden parts and the artist's correction. This is the proposed evidence base for learning toon behavior and powering future effects. Even a large goal such as 100,000 consented examples needs measured tests on unseen poses and characters; it cannot by itself guarantee convincing animation. Training runs outside the lightweight browser creator workflow.

Make the multi-angle training requirement explicit: **every authored camera angle gets its own 2D drawing in that perspective**. The artist polygon-selects visible body parts separately in each drawing, then assigns the same semantic IDs to their matching joints or regions in a shared 3D rig fitted for that view. Save camera, drawing, polygons, rig shape and pose, depth ordering, hidden-part markers and approved corrections as a linked example. Together, the examples show the model how one 3D character's parts should appear as different drawn perspectives; they do not ask it to pretend that a front-view image contains unseen side or back art.

**The reveal is playable.** The artist's flat old-timey cartoon fights in a 2.5D beat-'em-up layout. A special move tears open the paper body; the image lies ripped on the ground with an X in each eye while its stylized rigged counterpart crawls out. The world was 3D all along: the camera and controls now reveal a playable 3D fight with new directions, grabs, effects and stage interactions. The fighter eventually picks up its crumpled drawing, climbs back inside and resumes flat play. Both forms share the same identity and game state. The paper body is a return point and comic prop, not a duplicate player. Script the first example so the transition, contact and return are dependable.

To teach the proposed Toonworld Dynamics engine, pair many views of the same drawn character with their camera angles, polygon part identities, flexible rig shapes, contact points, depth ordering and artist-approved visual results. Different front, side and three-quarter examples teach what the 3D form should imply while preserving exaggerated 2D silhouettes. When a hand grips a world object, the model can propose which drawn fingers pass in front of or behind it from that camera. Begin with a supervised multi-view character and one successful grab; test free-form model-proposed effects only after the basic gameplay and corrections work.

Give authorized agents open, documented inspection and action surfaces: find a region; query its terrain, scale, water, protected cells and available support surfaces; inspect an asset's oriented footprint, base and doorway; ask for a placement plan; preview contact, collision, travel and lighting implications; commit atomically or undo. The same semantic, logical and mathematical constraints serve a programmer API and an agent API. An agent can do repeated assembly while the creator adjusts a landmass, accepts an asset or says “make the sea between them feel wider.” No LLM is required in the render loop.

With those footholds, agents could progressively create and implement more of a game using existing maps and assets: world layout, object placement, paths, gameplay references and later animations. This would be easier than forcing an agent to reconstruct the whole game from pictures. A full-game-from-book experience is an eventual goal that requires substantial new interpretation, validation and runtime work.

## Route into a game engine

Tiled and Blender are the first communities to serve. Godot is the first planned game-engine target after the import and labeling experience is reliable. Export or adapt terrain with numeric elevations, placed GLB instances, collision proxies, stable IDs and semantic anchors to a Godot starter project; let Godot handle scripts, gameplay and runtime scenes. Where Tiled JSON or glTF cannot express an ÆXIS rule directly, include a versioned companion manifest and document supported subsets. Keep the creator's source files and ÆXIS world editable after export.

**Proof of usefulness:** An author supplies a small map, adds a second region, labels the base and entrance of one Blender asset, has an agent propose a valid placement, previews it in the browser, undoes and revises it, then opens the matching starter scene in Godot. Source terrain, IDs, elevations and accepted labels survive. Measure this on the 4 GB target before advertising seamless handoff.

## Addendum: a Toonworld style for many characters

The training examples can teach more than one cartoon's motion. Pair each camera-specific 2D drawing and polygon-labeled body part with the fitted flexible 3D rig, then keep actions such as reach, grip, turn and recoil tied to semantic joint and contact roles. A compatible human character could reuse those actions through a mapped skeleton. A **camera-aware aesthetic mask** makes that character appear hand-drawn: it chooses or deforms the appropriate perspective image, shapes the silhouette and decides what appears before or behind a held 3D object. The spatial rig remains responsible for actually moving and holding that object.

Make skulls especially flexible. A human, muzzle-faced animal, beaked character or exaggerated cartoon may need a different head rig, but gaze, mouth, expression and neck connection can still be named roles. Body and face proportions may stretch beyond human measurements. A newly mapped or reshaped character enters a **calibration tool**: preview speaking, turning, grabbing, stretching, impacts and return to rest from the relevant camera angles. Flag lost hand contact, misplaced facial controls, odd occlusion, collisions and broken recovery, then let the artist fix and save an adaptation profile. Approved corrections may become new training pairs when the artist permits it. The torn-paper escape into the waiting 3D world tests the full behavior; a dependable cross-character grab is the first smaller proof. These capabilities remain proposals.
